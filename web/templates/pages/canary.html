What this server keeps

Everything you create here is stored in this market’s database and in the operator’s backups. It is kept until the operator removes it; there is no account or message deletion, except photos and avatars (below). The operator, anyone else who controls the server or database, and anyone holding a decrypted backup can read all of it. Each item below also says who can read it through the market.

  • Handle and role. Chosen at registration and cannot be changed. Shown to everyone you trade or message with, to moderators and administrators, and publicly with your listings and vendor page if you sell. Administrators see every account’s handle and role, and whether and when an account was suspended. Moderators and administrators are also listed publicly on this page, with their avatar, only while an administrator has turned the public staff list on and they have chosen to be listed.
  • Password. Stored only as a bcrypt hash; nobody can read the password through the market.
  • Second factors. The authenticator (TOTP) secret, encrypted with a key derived from the server’s setup token, so the operator can open it; recovery codes as hashes (newly issued codes are kept encrypted for about ten minutes until shown once); whether PGP sign-in is on. You and administrators see which factors are on.
  • PGP public key, its fingerprint, the date you proved you own it, when you last changed it and which key-expiry notice you were last sent. The key, fingerprint and proof date are shown to every signed-in account that looks up your handle, to everyone you trade with, and publicly on your vendor page if you sell; a buyer of your paid physical order also sees the date you changed or re-verified the key when that is after their order.
  • XMPP address. Shown only on your own account page.
  • Messages. The encrypted message (the server cannot decrypt it), who sent it to whom and when, and whether it was encrypted to the recipient’s key. The sender and recipient see it in Messages; moderators and administrators do not see messages through the market, except that while a dispute is open, staff who are not party to it see how many messages each party sent to each moderator or administrator since it opened and when the latest was sent, never what.
  • Notifications. Plain text such as “New message from” a handle, short order IDs with their new state, payment notes and payout transaction IDs. Only you see them.
  • Orders and drafts, including unpaid drafts and cancelled orders: listing, buyer, vendor, price and every state change with who made it and when, plus order notes such as the vendor’s note to the buyer and the cancellation reason. Seen by the buyer and vendor, and by moderators and administrators while the order is disputed or resolved, a payment on it is flagged for review or its escrow has an open staff alert (a settlement not seen by the network, or a release too small to build).
  • Disputes. The reason, the other party’s one reply (kept in the order history), the decision and the outcome, in plain text. Seen by the buyer, the vendor, moderators and administrators.
  • Reviews. Rating and text. Public on product and vendor pages, dated by month, without your handle; the vendor sees it with your handle on the order. Administrators can hide a review from the public pages; it is kept, not deleted, and still shown on the order and to administrators, and you are notified.
  • Digital deliveries and listings. Delivered content is stored unencrypted and seen by the buyer and vendor, and by moderators and administrators once the order is disputed. Listings are public; their automatic delivery content is seen only by the vendor. If an administrator takes a listing down, their reason is stored unencrypted and seen only by the vendor and administrators.
  • Photos and avatars. For a listing photo, a vendor’s avatar or a staff member’s avatar, only the re-encoded JPEG, its thumbnail, its dimensions, an optional description (listing photos only), who it belongs to and when it was added. The file you uploaded, its name and its metadata are never kept (location, camera and date among them). Listing photos and their descriptions are public to anyone while the listing is published; a vendor’s avatar is public while their vendor page is live. A staff avatar is public on this page (and on an administrator’s vendor page) only while an administrator has turned the public staff list on and that staff member has chosen to be listed; otherwise it is shown only beside that staff member’s dispute decisions, to the buyer and vendor of those orders and to moderators, and to administrators. Demoting a staff member removes their staff avatar and resets their staff list choice to hidden. Demoting a vendor to buyer, archiving a listing or suspending the account hides its images from the public but does not delete them. A normal browser may keep viewed images in its disk cache: it reuses them for a day without asking the server, and the files can stay until its cache is cleared. Tor Browser keeps them in memory only, until it closes (not yet tried in Tor Browser, A-199). Removing or replacing an image, or a staff takedown, deletes it from the database at once; copies remain in backups made before that and, until PostgreSQL reuses the space, in its data files and write-ahead log. If the operator restores a backup made before a removal, the image returns until it is removed again. ClamAV checks uploads for known malware; it does not review picture content. An upload it flags stores no image: your activity log records “Upload refused by the malware scanner”, and the server log keeps the signature name and the reference shown to the uploader, for as long as Docker’s logs are kept (3 × 10 MB rotation), and its time can be matched to the uploader’s activity-log row.
  • Payments. Deposit addresses, amounts, transaction IDs and confirmations. Seen by the buyer and vendor, by moderators and administrators while the order is disputed or resolved, a payment on it is flagged or its escrow has an open staff alert, and by administrators on payouts; transactions are also recorded on the {{if $.LiveNetwork}}mainnet blockchain{{else}}test network{{end}}.
  • Payout addresses. Saved on your account and copied onto each payout with its transaction ID. You see your addresses; administrators see them on payouts that need attention; the payout transaction ID only its recipient and staff reading the order, never the other party.
  • Bitcoin escrow. Only on a market that has run its Bitcoin escrow (test networks only). Your escrow key: its extended public key, sealed like the authenticator secret, an HMAC of it that stops two accounts registering the same key, its origin, whether it comes from Bitcoin Core or a per-order wallet (a vendor's active key type is shown to anyone on their listings and profile) and when you signed its practice transaction (the practice PSBT is not kept). For each escrow order: the escrow address, the three child public keys (the buyer’s, the vendor’s and the market’s) with each party’s own key origin and child path, the buyer’s refund address, the market fee and its destination, and each deposit. For a physical order’s escrow also the release deadline length and dispatch window it was created with, the time the buyer may cancel if it is not shipped, the release deadline and whether its reminder was sent; a physical listing keeps its dispatch window. For each settlement (the vendor’s claim, a refund or a stray refund): its destination, its outputs and amounts, the unsigned transaction (a PSBT) and its transaction ID, each signing party’s signature on each input (the pasted PSBT is not kept) and, once the market completes it, the final signed transaction, which is public once broadcast; the market-signed PSBT is never kept. A fee bump of a broadcast settlement (a replacement that pays a higher network fee from the claimant’s output) keeps the same: who asked for it, its fee rate and network fee, its unsigned transaction and transaction ID, each signing party’s signature on each input and, once completed, its final signed transaction. A deposit spent by a transaction this market did not make is flagged for staff, with that transaction’s ID when the market’s wallet names it, and addresses the watch-only wallet holds with no escrow on record are listed for administrators, with their public keys kept so that no new escrow uses them again (after a restore from backup each account’s lowest next escrow child key is kept too). All of it is kept until the operator removes it. The buyer and vendor see their order’s escrow, its deposits, the settlement outputs and, after the broadcast, the final transaction; each party sees only its own key origin, and the refund address only the buyer until a settlement that pays it lists its outputs; moderators and administrators see it while the order is disputed or resolved or a payment on it is flagged. Outside the database, the market’s Bitcoin node keeps every escrow’s public descriptor and address in its watch-only wallet, labelled with the order ID, with no age limit, so coins sent late can still be returned; settlement transactions are public on the test network the escrow runs on.
  • Monero escrow. Only on a market that has run its Monero escrow (a local test chain; custodial: the market holds the wallet keys and can move these coins). For each order paid into one: the escrow address (an account of the market’s monthly escrow wallet that is used only for that order), the buyer’s refund address, the market fee and its destination, and each deposit. For a physical order’s escrow also the release deadline length and dispatch window it was created with, the time the buyer may cancel if it is not shipped, the release deadline and whether its reminder was sent. For each settlement (the vendor’s claim, a refund or a stray refund): its destination, its outputs and amounts and the transaction the market built for it, with its transaction ID, network fee and the coins it spends, which the market stores before it sends it and never changes; why it waits or failed; and, for a failed one, an administrator’s request to build it again. For each monthly escrow wallet: its name, its primary address, the block height to restore it from, its seed encrypted to the operator’s offline OpenPGP key together with that key’s fingerprint, its state and its retention dates, and each of its accounts with the order that claimed it. The sealed seed is kept until the wallet is destroyed, 90 days after its last order closes, and is then wiped; the rest is kept until the operator removes it, and every database backup taken before then keeps the seed too. A deposit sent after an order closed is recorded and flagged, and returned to the buyer’s refund address until the wallet is destroyed. The buyer and vendor see their order’s escrow, its deposits and the settlement outputs; each output’s address is shown only to the party it pays, and the refund address only to the buyer. Moderators and administrators see it while the order is disputed or resolved or a payment on it is flagged, and administrators see settlements held after a restore and the wallets destroyed. Outside the database, the market’s escrow wallet-rpc keeps every wallet file, opened with a password derived from a secret in the operator’s configuration (no wallet password is stored in the database), until the operator deletes the files of a destroyed wallet; backups of those files keep copies until they expire. The coins and transactions exist only on that local test chain.
  • Activity log. Every sign-in, sign-out, account change and order action with its time, naming handles, key fingerprints, order IDs and transaction IDs. You see your latest entries on your account page; administrators see every account’s.

Temporary sign-in records (hashed session tokens, pending sign-ins, CAPTCHA answers and PGP challenges) expire within 12 hours. No email address is asked for.

The application records no IP address or browser details and keeps no access log. A reverse proxy, Tor service or hosting provider in front of it may log connections; ask the operator.

The signed audit export contains the activity log of every account, with handles. It is private operator evidence, never to be published (see the signing key below).

What this application currently does

  • Renders HTML and CSS on the server without requiring client-side JavaScript.
  • Supports account sessions with optional TOTP or PGP second factors, marketplace listings, and orders that move only through a server-enforced state machine.
  • Accepts only OpenPGP-encrypted messages and shows whether each was encrypted to the recipient's saved key. It does not encrypt or decrypt messages.
  • Marks a PGP key verified only after its holder signs or decrypts a server challenge.
  • When the operator runs the ClamAV malware scanner, accepts listing photos and avatars and stores them only as re-encoded JPEGs without metadata. ClamAV checks uploads for known malware; it does not review picture content.
    • {{if $.LiveNetwork}}
  • Accepts MAINNET Bitcoin or Monero payments into the operator's pooled wallet and pays releases and refunds from it. These are real funds; this is custodial holding, not escrow.
    • {{else}}
  • When the operator configures a test-network wallet, accepts TESTNET Bitcoin or Monero payments into the operator's pooled test wallet and pays releases and refunds from it. No real funds; {{if or $.EscrowOn $.XMREscrowOn}}for orders not paid into an escrow, this is custodial holding.{{else}}this is custodial holding, not escrow.{{end}}
    • {{end}}{{if $.EscrowOn}}
  • Bitcoin escrow ({{template "btc-escrow-only" $.EscrowNet}}): a digital listing with automatic delivery, or a physical listing with a dispatch window, can be paid into its own 2-of-3 multisig escrow of the buyer's, the vendor's and the market's keys, so the market cannot spend it alone, if the key each party's order page shows as theirs is theirs: each party checks the escrow descriptor in their own wallet, because a server that was taken over could show its own key in place of a party's. Coins leave it only by a settlement the market builds: one party signs it in their own wallet and the market co-signs and broadcasts it, or both parties sign it and the market broadcasts what they signed. It runs on {{template "btc-escrow-net" $.EscrowNet}} with no real funds, and is not verified on any public network.
    • {{end}}{{if $.XMREscrowOn}}
  • Monero escrow ({{if eq $.XMREscrowNet "stagenet"}}Monero stagenet, a public test network{{else}}local fakechain only{{end}}): an order in Monero can be paid into an escrow account of its own, and its coins are {{$.XMRCustody}}. The market, not the parties, controls these coins: it builds and sends each release or refund from the account, so you must trust the market with them. It runs on a Monero fakechain, a local test chain with no real funds, and is not verified on any public network.
    • {{end}}
  • Shows a warrant canary only as the operator's PGP-clearsigned text, re-verified against the configured operator key on every page view; it never writes or edits canary text.
