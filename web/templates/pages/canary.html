What this server keeps

{{template "retention:summary" .}}

What you create here is stored in this market’s database and in the operator’s backups. {{if and .Retention .Retention.Enabled}}The retention rules above apply, subject to preservation and financial safety exceptions.{{else}}Most records are kept until the operator removes them; there is no account or message deletion, except photos and avatars (below).{{end}} Form submission receipts expire and are swept as described below. The operator, anyone else who controls the server or database, and anyone holding a decrypted backup can read retained plaintext and metadata and obtain ciphertext. Proper recipient-encrypted content requires a recipient private key to decrypt. Each item below also says who can read it through the market.

{{if and .Access .Access.MembersOnly}}

On this members-only site, references below to public content mean content available to signed-in accounts. The listing, account and role restrictions described below still apply.

{{end}}
  • Form submission receipts. For a new listing, encrypted message or ordinary order state change, the server keeps a random form identifier, your account, a hash of your session token, a keyed hash of the submitted fields, the action path, redirect and success notice. The receipt does not keep the submitted text, an IP address or browser details. It lets the same account and session recover the original success when the same form is submitted again unchanged. It expires after 24 hours; a running server sweeps expired receipts at startup and every minute in bounded batches. A stopped server or a backup may keep them longer. No account can read receipts through the market.
  • Handle and role. Chosen at registration and cannot be changed. Shown to everyone you trade or message with, to moderators and administrators, and publicly with your listings and vendor page if you sell. Administrators see every account’s handle and role, and whether and when an account was suspended. Moderators and administrators are also listed publicly on this page, with their avatar, only while an administrator has turned the public staff list on and they have chosen to be listed.
  • Vendor applications. Each application’s status, the bond and approval rule it was made under, when and by which administrator the bond was recorded or waived and the application decided, and the administrator’s note. You see your latest on your account; administrators see open applications. A verified-vendor mark records the administrator who last changed it. Erasing your account removes your applications.
  • Vendor bonds paid in Monero. When the market gives an application a bond address, that address and each payment to it (transaction ID, amount, confirmations, and whether it counted, arrived late, was locked or later fell below the confirmations needed); the bond wallet’s seed is kept encrypted to the operator’s key. You see your address and totals on your account; administrators see them too. Erasing your account removes your applications but keeps the address and its payments, with nothing naming your account, as the record of coins the market holds.
  • Account bans. If an administrator bans an account (permanent closure), when, which administrator, the reason they gave and the time of any earlier suspension; and the account’s handle in lower case, so it is never registered again. Administrators see it; the reason is also in the audit trail of both accounts, so a banned account sees it in its own activity only if the ban is lifted. Lifting the ban clears the ban record and the handle reservation; closing the account clears the ban record with the profile, and the reserved handle stays after the account is erased. The audit rows that carry the reason follow the audit trail’s retention.
  • Password. Stored only as a bcrypt hash; nobody can read the password through the market.
  • Second factors. The authenticator (TOTP) secret, encrypted with a key derived from the server’s application secret (setup token on a legacy installation), so the operator can open it; recovery codes as hashes (newly issued codes are kept encrypted for about ten minutes until shown once); whether PGP sign-in is on. You and administrators see which factors are on.
  • PGP public key, its fingerprint, the date you proved you own it, when you last changed it and which key-expiry notice you were last sent. The key, fingerprint and proof date are shown to market staff, to everyone you trade with or have messaged, to every signed-in account if you sell, appear on the public staff list or are named as the staff contact, to both parties of every disputed or resolved order if you are a moderator or administrator, and publicly on your vendor page if you sell; a buyer of your paid physical order also sees the date you changed or re-verified the key when that is after their order.
  • Messages. The encrypted message (the server cannot decrypt it), who sent it to whom and when, whether it was encrypted to the recipient’s key, and the send-time count of other visible recipient key IDs when measurable. The count adds no stored key IDs beyond those already inside the ciphertext. The sender and recipient see it in Messages; moderators and administrators do not see messages through the market, except that while a dispute is open, staff who are not party to it see how many messages each party sent to each moderator or administrator since it opened and when the latest was sent, never what.
  • Notifications. Plain text such as “New message from” a handle, short order IDs with their new state, payment notes and payout transaction IDs. New order-specific notices also retain that order’s full ID to link to its record; older notices and account-only notices may have no link. Only you see your notifications; opening an order still requires permission to read it.
  • Orders and drafts, including unpaid drafts and cancelled orders: listing, buyer, vendor, price and every state change with who made it and when, plus order notes such as the vendor’s note to the buyer and the cancellation reason. When a draft first requests payment, the listing title, full description and fulfillment kind are copied into the order and retained with it, including in backups; later listing edits or archiving do not remove that copy. Orders with no copy say that their listing details are unavailable instead of claiming the current listing was what was purchased. The current listing remains a separate link. These records are seen by the buyer and vendor, and by moderators and administrators while the order is disputed or resolved or has a dispute record from a party concession, a payment on it is flagged for review or its escrow has an open staff alert (a settlement not seen by the network, or a release too small to build).
  • Disputes. The reason, the other party’s one reply (kept in the order history), the decision and the outcome, in plain text. Seen by the buyer, the vendor, moderators and administrators.
  • Reviews. Rating and text. Public on product and vendor pages, dated by month, without your handle; the vendor sees it with your handle on the order. Administrators can hide a review from the public pages; it is kept, not deleted, and still shown on the order and to administrators, and you are notified.
  • Digital deliveries and listings. Delivered content is stored unencrypted and seen by the buyer and vendor, and by moderators and administrators once the order is disputed. Listings are public; their automatic delivery content is seen only by the vendor. If an administrator takes a listing down, their reason is stored unencrypted and seen only by the vendor and administrators.
  • Market name and logo. The saved name and current logo are public, including before sign-in on a members-only site. A logo is stored as a normalized JPEG or a restricted static SVG, with its dimensions, version, updating administrator and time. Raster metadata is removed; SVG visible text, presentation and supported accessibility labels may remain. Replacing or removing the logo deletes the prior live image, but downloaded copies, backups, database pages and write-ahead logs can retain it. Temporary logo-upload authorizations bind the administrator and session, keep a token hash and sealed token for the private handoff, expire after 10 minutes and are cleaned up by the authentication expiry worker. Audit records describe authorization, replacement and reset without storing image bytes.
  • Photos and avatars. For a listing photo, a vendor’s avatar or a staff member’s avatar, only the re-encoded JPEG, its thumbnail, its dimensions, an optional description (listing photos only), who it belongs to and when it was added. The file you uploaded, its name and its metadata are never kept (location, camera and date among them). Listing photos and their descriptions are {{if and .Access .Access.MembersOnly}}visible to signed-in accounts{{else}}public to anyone{{end}} while the listing is published; a vendor’s avatar is {{if and .Access .Access.MembersOnly}}visible to signed-in accounts{{else}}public{{end}} while their vendor page is live. A staff avatar is {{if and .Access .Access.MembersOnly}}visible to signed-in accounts{{else}}public{{end}} on this page (and on an administrator’s vendor page) only while an administrator has turned the public staff list on and that staff member has chosen to be listed; otherwise it is shown only beside that staff member’s dispute decisions, to the buyer and vendor of those orders and to moderators, and to administrators. Demoting a staff member removes their staff avatar and resets their staff list choice to hidden. Demoting a vendor to buyer, archiving a listing or suspending the account hides its images from the public but does not delete them. {{if and .Access .Access.MembersOnly}}Members-only image responses require a valid signed-in account and tell browsers not to store them (no-store). Restricting access cannot recall images already downloaded or cached.{{else}}A normal browser may keep viewed images in its disk cache: it reuses them for a day without asking the server, and the files can stay until its cache is cleared. Tor Browser keeps them in memory only, until it closes (not yet tried in Tor Browser, A-199).{{end}} Removing or replacing an image, or a staff takedown, deletes it from the database at once; copies remain in backups made before that and, until PostgreSQL reuses the space, in its data files and write-ahead log. If the operator restores a backup made before a removal, the image returns until it is removed again. ClamAV checks uploads for known malware; it does not review picture content. An upload it flags stores no image: your activity log records “Upload refused by the malware scanner”, and the server log keeps the signature name and the reference shown to the uploader, for as long as Docker’s logs are kept (3 × 10 MB rotation), and its time can be matched to the uploader’s activity-log row.
  • Payments. Monero hides sender, recipient and amount information on-chain; this does not hide those details from the market or prevent traffic correlation. Deposit addresses, amounts, transaction IDs and confirmations. Both parties see amounts, confirmations and payment status. Both parties see escrow deposit identifiers for verification; transactions are also recorded on the {{if $.LiveNetwork}}mainnet blockchain{{else}}test network{{end}}.
  • External escrow address reservations. Your account, the currency and each external refund or claim address you used are kept in the database and backups to prevent you reusing that address. Other accounts cannot look up these records, and checkout or claims on unrelated orders do not reveal your prior external address use on other orders. Market-owned addresses and the buyer's refund address on the same order remain forbidden claim destinations.
  • Payout addresses. A payout address saved on your account before escrow is still stored with your account, but this market no longer reads or shows it and pays nothing to it. Escrow settlements pay the addresses described below.
  • Monero escrow. Only on a market that has run its Monero escrow ({{if $.XMREscrowOn}}{{template "payment-status:xmr-on" $}}{{else if $.LiveNetwork}}Monero mainnet, with real funds{{else}}a Monero test network{{end}}; custodial: the market holds the wallet keys and can move these coins). For each order paid into one: the escrow address (an account of the market’s monthly escrow wallet that is used only for that order), the buyer’s refund address, the market fee and its destination, and each deposit. For a physical order’s escrow also the release deadline length and dispatch window it was created with, the time the buyer may cancel if it is not shipped, the release deadline and whether its reminder was sent. For each settlement (the vendor’s claim, a refund or a stray refund): its destination, its outputs and amounts and the transaction the market built for it, with its transaction ID, network fee and the coins it spends, which the market stores before it sends it and never changes; why it waits or failed; and, for a failed one, an administrator’s request to build it again. For each monthly escrow wallet: its name, its primary address, the block height to restore it from, its seed encrypted to the operator’s offline OpenPGP key together with that key’s fingerprint, its state and its retention dates, and each of its accounts with the order that claimed it. The sealed seed is kept until the wallet is destroyed, 90 days after its last order closes, and is then wiped; the rest is kept until the operator removes it, and every database backup taken before then keeps the seed too. A deposit sent after an order closed is recorded and flagged, and returned to the buyer’s refund address until the wallet is destroyed. The buyer and vendor see their order’s escrow, its deposits and the settlement outputs; each output’s address is shown only to the party it pays, and the refund address only to the buyer. Moderators and administrators see it while the order is disputed or resolved or has a dispute record from a party concession or a payment on it is flagged, and administrators see settlements held after a restore and the wallets destroyed. Outside the database, the market’s escrow wallet-rpc keeps every wallet file, opened with a password derived from a secret in the operator’s configuration (no wallet password is stored in the database), until the operator deletes the files of a destroyed wallet; backups of those files keep copies until they expire. {{if eq $.XMREscrowNet "mainnet"}}The coins and transactions are on Monero mainnet: they are real funds.{{else}}The coins and transactions exist only on that test network.{{end}}
  • Activity log. Every sign-in, sign-out, account change, message sent, mark-all notification read, and order action is recorded with its time and the identifiers that action records (handles, key fingerprints, order IDs and transaction IDs). Message-send entries do not name the recipient. Marking all notifications read is recorded; reading one notification is not. You see your latest entries on your account page; administrators see every account’s.

Temporary sign-in records (hashed session tokens, pending sign-ins, CAPTCHA answers and PGP challenges) stop working within 12 hours and are deleted later. No email address is asked for.

The application records no IP address or browser details and keeps no access log. A reverse proxy, Tor service or hosting provider in front of it may log connections; ask the operator.

The signed audit export contains the activity log of every account, with handles. It is private operator evidence, never to be published (see the signing key below).

What this application currently does

  • Renders HTML and CSS on the server without requiring client-side JavaScript.
  • Supports account sessions with optional TOTP or PGP second factors, marketplace listings, and orders that move only through a server-enforced state machine.
  • Accepts only OpenPGP-encrypted messages that name the recipient’s usable saved key, and shows whether each was. It refuses a message to an account without a usable key, a passphrase-only message and one that names only other keys; one whose recipient key IDs are all hidden is accepted only after the sender confirms, and is marked as recipient unknown. It does not encrypt or decrypt messages.
  • Marks a PGP key verified only after its holder signs or decrypts a server challenge.
  • When the operator runs the ClamAV malware scanner, accepts listing photos and avatars and stores them only as re-encoded JPEGs without metadata. ClamAV checks uploads for known malware; it does not review picture content.
  • New payments use Monero escrow only when the operator enables and verifies the escrow service.
    • {{if $.XMREscrowOn}}
