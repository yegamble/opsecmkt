Reset a user's second factors

{{with $.NoticeIn "reset-factors"}}
{{.}}
{{end}}

For a user who lost their authenticator and recovery codes, or their PGP private key. This turns off TOTP (deleting its secret and recovery codes) and PGP sign-in verification; their saved PGP key and its verification stay. They are signed out everywhere and notified.{{if eq (index .Settings "allow_login_without_pgp") "true"}} They sign in next with their password only.{{else}} This market still requires PGP decryption at sign-in. Resetting the optional second factors cannot replace it; use identity-verified replacement-key recovery below. The replacement key must then be decrypted; the login policy stays in force.{{end}}

When the account holder can still use their verified PGP key, ask them to sign a fresh message and verify it against the key already saved on their account. Do not accept a replacement key merely because it was supplied with the reset request; verify identity out of band first. If they cannot use that key, follow Replace a lost PGP key in the operator guide (docs/operator-guide.md#replace-a-lost-pgp-key) before resetting.

Recover a lost PGP key

Optional privileged recovery, following Replace a lost PGP key in the operator guide (docs/operator-guide.md#replace-a-lost-pgp-key). If the holder can still use the saved key, have them sign a fresh text you choose and verify the signature offline against the fingerprint on the account's page; if the key is lost, the market cannot check who they are, so accept only the evidence the guide describes. This replaces their public-key binding even when no optional second factor is enrolled, resets optional factors, ends every session and pending sign-in, and leaves the new key unverified. The login policy stays in force; strict sign-in still requires decryption with the new key.

Required with a replacement key: 10–500 characters on one line saying how you checked the request, for example which text was signed and how you verified it. It is your attestation, which the market cannot check, recorded with the old and new fingerprints and your administrator handle in the audit trail of both accounts. The note goes into the audit trail and the signed audit export. Do not put the holder's real-world identity, contact details or other private evidence in it.

Supply a usable public encryption key only. Never paste or upload a private key.

Check the request really comes from the account holder first: anyone who knows their password can then sign in.{{if ne (index .Settings "allow_login_without_pgp") "true"}} PGP decryption is still required while this market's strict policy is on.{{end}}

Suspend or restore an account

{{with $.NoticeIn "suspend"}}
{{.}}
{{end}}

Contains a compromised or abusive account. Suspending signs it out everywhere, cancels any sign-in in progress and refuses new sign-ins until you restore it. Its role, listings and orders stay as they are. Orders waiting on this account stop until it is restored; the other party can still complete, cancel where allowed, or open a dispute. A suspended moderator does not resolve disputes, is not notified of them or of payment reviews and is not offered as a dispute contact. The account is notified and both accounts record the change.

Restoring hands the account back to whoever has its password. Verify that the password holder should regain access before you restore it.

Ban or unban an account

{{with $.NoticeIn "ban"}}
{{.}}
{{end}}

A ban is permanent closure, for an account that must not use this market again; a suspension is the reversible hold above. Banning signs the account out everywhere and refuses its sign-in, archives its active listings, withdraws an open vendor application, removes a verified vendor mark, and stops new orders and messages to it. Its handle stays reserved for good, even after retention erases the account, so nobody can register it again. A moderator also becomes a buyer. Its open orders keep going: the other party can still complete, concede or dispute them, an administrator who is not a party decides disputes, refunds are still paid, and a release to the banned vendor is held until the ban is lifted (an administrator may refund one held longer than the period under Payments to the buyer instead, from its order page). The account is notified, and both accounts record the change with your reason.

Lifting a ban lets whoever has the password sign in again, unless the account was suspended before the ban: that suspension comes back. Its listings stay archived, the verified mark stays off and no staff role returns: the vendor publishes listings again, and an administrator decides whether to verify them or assign a role again.

Action
Required for a ban and for lifting one: 10–500 characters on one line. It is kept with the ban, shown to administrators here and recorded in the audit trail of both accounts; the account sees it in its own activity only if the ban is lifted.
