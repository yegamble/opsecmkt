Reset a user's second factors

{{with $.NoticeIn "reset-factors"}}
{{.}}
{{end}}

For a user who lost their authenticator and recovery codes, or their PGP private key. This turns off TOTP (deleting its secret and recovery codes) and PGP sign-in verification; their saved PGP key and its verification stay. They are signed out everywhere, notified, and sign in next with their password only.

When the account holder can still use their verified PGP key, ask them to sign a fresh message and verify it against the key already saved on their account. Do not trust a replacement key supplied with the reset request. If they cannot use that key, follow the operator’s account-recovery procedure before resetting.

Suspend or restore an account

{{with $.NoticeIn "suspend"}}
{{.}}
{{end}}

Contains a compromised or abusive account. Suspending signs it out everywhere, cancels any sign-in in progress and refuses new sign-ins until you restore it. Its role, listings and orders stay as they are. Orders waiting on this account stop until it is restored; the other party can still complete, cancel where allowed, or open a dispute. A suspended moderator does not resolve disputes, is not notified of them or of payment reviews and is not offered as a dispute contact. The account is notified and both accounts record the change.

Restoring hands the account back to whoever has its password. Verify that the password holder should regain access before you restore it.

Ban or unban an account

{{with $.NoticeIn "ban"}}
{{.}}
{{end}}

A ban is permanent closure, for an account that must not use this market again; a suspension is the reversible hold above. Banning signs the account out everywhere and refuses its sign-in, archives its active listings, withdraws an open vendor application, removes a verified vendor mark, and stops new orders and messages to it. Its handle stays reserved, so nobody can register it again. Its open orders keep going: the other party can still complete, concede or dispute them, an administrator who is not a party decides disputes, and releases and refunds are still paid (a release that still needs a banned vendor's payout address waits until the ban is lifted). The account is notified, and both accounts record the change with your reason.

Lifting a ban lets whoever has the password sign in again. Its listings stay archived and the verified mark stays off: the vendor publishes listings again, and an administrator decides whether to verify them again.

Action
Required for a ban and for lifting one: 10–500 characters on one line. It is kept with the ban, shown to administrators here and recorded in the audit trail of both accounts; the account sees it in its own activity only if the ban is lifted.
