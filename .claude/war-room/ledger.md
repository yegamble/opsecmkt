##### C3/D1 — drain scope on refusal

- DECISION: **MODIFY (drain every refusal on the upload route, never into memory)**. Order on `/listings/photo` and `/account/avatar`: method + Origin/Sec-Fetch-Site → `Content-Length` required (absent/chunked → 411 unread) and ≤ **cap + 4 MiB** (else 413 unread; reset accepted, documented) → session lookup under `takeLookupSlot` → sign-in, role vendor|admin, `write:` and `upload:` budgets, `uploadWork` slot, one-per-account lock → deadlines extended **only now** → first part `csrf` (≤128 B) → `id`, optional `description` → `photo` via `LimitReader(cap+1)`. Every refusal at any step copies the remainder to `io.Discard` up to the ceiling under the deadline in force (unextended 15 s before session+role; the route deadline after), then renders the HTML page via `httpError.Render` (as `adminHandleError` does), never `text/plain`. Beyond ceiling or deadline the reset stands.
- WHY: UI measured Firefox JS-off `NS_ERROR_NET_RESET` 4/4 without a drain and the 413 page 4/4 with one. The four seats objected to memory, slots and deadline extension for unauthenticated clients; `io.Discard`, no slot and the unextended 15 s satisfy all three, so an expired-session vendor gets "Sign in again" instead of a reset. A slow anonymous client already holds a connection 15 s today; the drain adds nothing the attacker does not pay byte-for-byte.
- DISSENTING VIEW: PRIV, SEC, DA, SRE — cut anonymous/expired/buyer early, drain only for a live vendor/admin session. Falsification: `TestUploadAnonDrainHoldsNoSlot` showing a slot, buffer or deadline extension → revert to the early cut.
- USER IMPACT: every refusal is a readable page stating the limit, listing form kept.
- OPERATOR IMPACT: none.

### Owner directive (2026-09-29) — per-order escrow replaces the pooled wallet (WR-escrow)

The owner directed a payments redesign: instead of pooling coins in one market wallet (custodial, exposed to an exit scam or a single compromise), each order gets its own escrow that the buyer pays within a set time. Digital (auto-delivery) orders resolve automatically on funding with the delivered note; physical orders keep funds in escrow until shipped/confirmed; buyer and vendor talk on the order page; in a dispute an administrator decides refund to the buyer or release to the vendor. The network fee is deducted from the escrowed amount (refund included); the market's fee from a fulfilled order goes to an administrator-configured address per coin. Owner answers to the chair's questions (2026-09-29): **(1) Bitcoin keys: buyer, vendor and market** — true non-custodial 2-of-3 (buyer and vendor register a key/xpub from their own wallet; the market holds only the arbitrator key; signing by pasting PSBTs into HTML forms, no JavaScript); **(2) Monero: per-order wallets now** (custodial per order, compartmentalised, no pool), 2-of-3 multisig evaluated later; **(3) pooled mode: keep until escrow ships, then remove** — safety fixes only in the pooled system, no new pooled features, removal with an upgrade path for open orders once escrow is verified end to end on regtest/testnet; **(4) auto-delivery: the market co-signs the vendor's claim** — on funding the content is delivered and the order completes; the vendor claims whenever they like by signing a PSBT the server co-signs automatically with the market key (kept hot for this; the server alone still cannot move funds). OPEN QUESTION (chair, for the council and then the owner): the owner wrote that physical-order funds stay in escrow "until the product is shipped and marked as shipped by the user" — either the vendor marks it shipped and the market co-signs the release (vendor paid at shipping; less buyer protection), or the buyer confirms receipt and signs the release (with a timeout and the dispute path as backstops); the council recommends, the owner decides. Process: a war-room design council (WR-escrow, Rounds 0–D with real regtest/stagenet-fork feasibility spikes) rules the phased design before any implementation; invariants 2 and 4 in repo-map.md updated.


### WR-escrow Round D ruling (chair, 2026-09-29) — architect, payments, security, privacy, buyer/vendor; Rounds 0, A, B+C

Evidence: Round 0 architecture pack and real-chain spikes, five Round A reports and five Round B+C replies (chair scratchpad `wr-escrow/`), each seat's own probes on Bitcoin Core 31.1 regtest and Monero 0.18.5.1 regtest (real local chain, NOT retained under A-103). Proven on regtest this round: a comma in a key field makes a 2-of-4 one party spends alone (SEC); a market signature made with SIGHASH_NONE lets the vendor redirect a refund and passes `testmempoolaccept` (SEC); a later market-signed outcome RBF-replaces an earlier broadcast one (PAYE P1); the watch-only wallet builds a PSBT over a spent outpoint (P2); a forged entry under the market pubkey merged into the stored copy blocks signing (SEC); `sendrawtransaction` of the same tx is idempotent (P3); a reorg + double-spend drops the txid from `listreceivedbyaddress` (P5); a Monero `do_not_relay` build does not lock outputs and a rebuilt sweep pays a late deposit to the vendor (P6); Monero per-order accounts: 300 accounts polled in one refresh + `get_transfers{all_accounts}` (0.3 s) and a spend beyond one account's balance refused with -17 (P7); the arbiter descriptor wallet signs at index 5000/19000 after `keypoolrefill` (ARCH, PAYE, SEC, PRIV); `rpcwhitelist` refuses key export while allowing signing (SEC); a practice PSBT is checkable with throwaway keys and never broadcastable (BUY). NOT proven: Sparrow, Electrum and hardware-wallet export/import/signing (E1); real-chain Monero account scan cost at 10k accounts (PAYE-E2); `keypoolrefill` under `rpcwhitelist` and on an encrypted wallet.

**Ruled design (binding for implementers; every item below is an acceptance criterion of the phase that builds it):**
- **ES-1 Settlement model (C1 + ARCH).** No new order states. Settlement is its own table with CAS states (building → awaiting_signature → party_signed → signed → broadcast → confirmed | external | failed) and order_events notes. The database refuses BOTH a second outcome per order (`UNIQUE(order_id) WHERE kind IN ('release','refund')`; `stray_refund` is its own kind) AND a second claim of any outpoint (`payments.settlement_id` claimed by CAS; `payments` stays the per-UTXO ledger — PAYE-8's separate table declined). Both terminal-transition call sites dispatch through one `settleTerminal` (ARCH-1); an escrow order never gets a `payouts` row and the send claim excludes escrow orders. Transitions stay RPC-free; the watcher builds, co-signs and broadcasts (ARCH-7).
- **ES-2 Signing pipeline (C2 + SEC-2 + PAYE-2/3).** The server builds every settlement itself (explicit confirmed, unspent inputs checked with `gettxout`; no wallet coin selection; outputs from the frozen plan; explicit fee rate; absolute and rate fee caps). A pasted PSBT is accepted only if its unsigned txid equals the stored one, it carries a signature on every input from the expected party's derived key (never the market key; an entry under the market key is refused), and every signature ends in 0x01; unknown/global/proprietary fields are ignored and the pasted blob is never stored or re-served. The market signs its pristine stored copy with `"ALL"` hard-coded, only in a terminal order state, only from the watcher; then `combinepsbt([market_signed, pasted])` market copy first; the final tx is re-checked (txid, outputs, fee ≤ cap, every witness signature 0x01), `testmempoolaccept` with explicit `maxfeerate`, persisted raw before the first broadcast, and only that stored tx is ever rebroadcast (Bitcoin "txid returned" and -27 are success). Never rebuild a settlement that has a signed or broadcast attempt. Only the server broadcasts; a market-signed PSBT is never shown to anyone (BUY-13 retracted); after broadcast the raw tx may be shown. Fee bumps (same recipients, only the claimant's output shrinks) are Phase 2; Phase 1 relies on recipient CPFP.
- **ES-3 Keys (C3 + C4 + K2 + K4).** One key expression per party, parsed strictly in Go before any RPC (`[fp/path]tpub…` with origin; refuse commas, parentheses, `#`, whitespace, any `prv`, WIF or word lists — a pasted private key gets "this key is exposed, replace it"). BIP48 paths accepted plainly; other paths only with a "wallet used only for this market" acknowledgement (Core exports only 44/49/84/86h accounts); SLIP-132 single-sig prefixes refused. Registration and change need `confirmedTx` step-up, notify, and never affect existing escrows. The account xpub is sealed with the keyRoot sealer; cross-user reuse is refused by `UNIQUE(HMAC(xpub))`. Per order the server expands child keys statelessly (`createpsbt` + `utxoupdatepsbt [xpub descriptor]`), stores only the three bare child pubkeys (`escrow_pubkeys(pubkey PRIMARY KEY)`) plus each party's own origin, imports only a bare-key `wsh(sortedmulti(2,…))` into the watch-only wallet, and checks the composed witness script (3 distinct compressed keys, exactly one derived from the arbiter fingerprint read from Core) before showing the address. A fresh market child index per escrow from a database counter; `escrows.address` UNIQUE. The arbiter key lives in a dedicated Core descriptor wallet; the app's RPC user runs under `rpcwhitelist` (escrow methods, `keypoolrefill`, no key export, dump or backup); the watcher refills the keypool ahead of the index high-water mark; startup pins the arbiter fingerprint and disables escrow on a mismatch. No market xpub or vendor account xpub is ever published (fingerprint pin only — SEC-5's canary xpub and PGP-published vendor xpub declined as privacy harms). Each party sees and downloads only its own origin (others bare); per-party PSBT copies via `utxoupdatepsbt`. **E1 result and OWNER DECISION 2026-09-29 ("Core now, Electrum later"):** Bitcoin Core signs this shape; Electrum 4.5.8/4.8.2 cannot (it signs only a 2-of-3 wallet built from three per-order Vpubs). Phase 1 supports Core signing only; the schema stores each party's full derivation suffix and an optional per-order xpub (no bare `/0/i` index column) so an Electrum/Sparrow shape can follow without a corrective migration (A-255).
- **ES-4 Who signs.** A settlement's outputs are frozen and pinned; the server accepts the one signature from EITHER escrow party (whoever signs first) and then co-signs; if both parties sign, the market does not sign. Digital orders: completion on funding (escrow-only system edge delivered→completed), then the vendor claims. Refunds, dispute outcomes and stray refunds take the same path. Dispute decisions on escrow orders are **administrator-only with `confirmedTx` step-up** (SEC-8; the owner's directive says "administrator"; ARCH retracted ARCH-4; a `roleAdmin` edge behind the escrow guard map) and cannot change once a market signature exists.
- **ES-5 Destinations and fees (C8 + PRIV-2 + PAYE-6).** The buyer's refund address is required and pinned at checkout (refused if the market already used it as an escrow, settlement or fee destination); the vendor's destination is taken when the settlement is built (fresh, single-use), never at signing. Fee bps and the fee destination are snapshotted at checkout; the network fee comes from the claimant's output only; the market-fee output is never reduced; outputs below dust/floor fold into the claimant's output with an order note; one floor per coin, no parallel copy (ARCH-14). The fee destination setting accepts a plain address or a watch-only descriptor (a fresh address per settlement via `deriveaddresses` on a counter); **OWNER DECISION 2026-09-29: descriptor on mainnet** — on a mainnet market the fee destination must be a watch-only descriptor (fresh fee address per settlement, index frozen in the settlement); a single fixed address is allowed on test networks with a warning.
- **ES-6 Restore and reconciliation (C7 + PAYE-4).** The restore gate also pauses market co-signing, Monero relay and new builds; watching continues. Reconciliation reads the chain (outpoints spent by an unknown txid → `external`, flagged; escrows in the wallet but not the database → orphan rows for staff; signed attempts rebroadcast only after the operator confirms).
- **ES-7 Monero (C6 + K1 + K8).** Escrow wallets run on their own monero-wallet-rpc, driven only by the watcher under its advisory lock; checkout claims a pre-created slot by database CAS and makes no RPC. Every spend names the escrow's `account_index` (never 0, never absent) after checking the open wallet's primary address; settlement is one transaction with `subtract_fee_from_outputs`, stored and relayed with `relay_tx`, never rebuilt. Wallet passwords come from a dedicated `MONERO_WALLET_SECRET` (never keyRoot or SETUP_TOKEN — rotating those must never lock funds); each wallet's seed is PGP-encrypted to the operator key and stored in the database at creation. Custody copy everywhere: "held by this market in a wallet used only for this order; the market can move these coins" — never "escrow the market cannot spend". **OWNER DECISION 2026-09-29: accounts in monthly wallets** — one escrow wallet per month holding one account per order (account 0 never used and kept empty; every spend names the order's account), each monthly wallet destroyed (file and sealed seed) 90 days after its last order closes; late deposits within that window refunded automatically; per-order wallet files (and ARCH-9 scheduling, SEC-4's serializer as a BLOCKER) are not built. A later Monero multisig evaluation would need per-order wallet files again.
- **ES-8 Retention and late funds (K9).** Bitcoin escrows stay imported (bare keys) and are checked daily plus on demand by a buyer "Recover coins at this escrow" action (buyer + market, auto co-sign) — no age limit. Monero: late deposits within the retention window are refunded to the pinned address automatically; after it the page says "Monero sent to this order's address after <date> cannot be recovered".
- **ES-9 Key activation (K6).** A key is inactive until its owner signs a practice PSBT: a reserved child index never used for escrows, throwaway server keys (never the market key), a fabricated prevout checked absent with `gettxout` (never broadcastable); offered only while the key has no escrows; nothing stored but "active at <time>". Required before any escrow is created, from Phase 1.
- **ES-10 Honest copy and privacy (SEC-5 revised, PRIV-6/7/10, BUY-10).** The Bitcoin claim is qualified ("the market cannot spend your escrow without you or the other party, if the key shown is yours; checking the descriptor in your wallet is what detects a substituted key"); the order page states the counterparty can trace funding and settlement on chain; audit and order-event text is fixed (no keys, fingerprints, destinations, PSBTs); RPC errors are mapped to fixed classes before logging; `/canary#records` gains the escrow records; docs per A-237's documentation criterion.
- **ES-11 Physical orders (OPEN QUESTION → owner).** Council 5/5 (SEC-14's buyer-signature variant withdrawn): release when the buyer clicks "Confirm receipt" (password step-up; authorises the market co-sign of the vendor's claim) or when a per-order auto-finalize deadline after "shipped" passes with no dispute (buyer notified at shipped and 72 h before; a dispute stops the clock); never release at "shipped"; a buyer may cancel and be refunded (buyer + market) if the vendor has not shipped within the listing's dispatch window plus grace. **OWNER DECISION 2026-09-29 (against the council's recommendation): the buyer must sign the release**, with a timeout — a physical order releases when the buyer signs the release PSBT in their own wallet together with the vendor (buyer + vendor; the market does not sign), or, if the buyer has not signed and no dispute is open, when the per-order auto-release deadline passes (then vendor + market, the market co-signing automatically); **deadline: 14 days after "shipped" by default, administrator-adjustable within bounds (7–30 days), snapshotted per order** (buyer notified at shipped and 72 h before; a dispute stops the clock). Never release at "shipped". Consequences for Phase 2: the buyer's release page is a PSBT signing page like the vendor's (same ES-2 checks, expected-outputs table, paste-keeping errors); ES-9 practice-sign matters more (a buyer who cannot sign falls back to the deadline, not to a stuck order); the vendor-cancel/no-ship refund path is unchanged.
